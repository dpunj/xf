# Posture Reset — From Fidgeting to Organized

A practical guide for breaking the hypersomatic posture-chasing cycle.
Based on Becoming a Supple Leopard (Starrett).

## The Vicious Cycle

```
 feel "off" ──► adjust shoulders ──► still feels wrong
     ▲                                       │
     │                                       ▼
 more tension ◄── adjust hips/back ◄── overcorrect
```

Fidgeting with individual joints without a system = chasing your tail.
Each adjustment displaces tension somewhere else. The nervous system
never finds "home."

## The Virtuous Cycle

```
 feel "off" ──► bracing sequence (once) ──► leave it alone
     ▲                                            │
     │                                            ▼
 neutral gets    ◄── mobilize restrictions ◄── tension fades
 easier over time         (later)               naturally
```

Posture isn't something you hold through willpower. It's something that
happens naturally when your tissues have the range to support it.

## Step 1: The Bracing Sequence (Your Reset Button)

Instead of adjusting parts independently, run through this **once** and
then stop touching it:

| Step | Cue | Why |
|------|-----|-----|
| 1 | Squeeze glutes | Sets pelvis in neutral — stops the arch/tuck hunt |
| 2 | Pull ribcage down | Locks ribcage to pelvis — one unit, not two |
| 3 | Belly tight (~20%) | Light engagement, not max brace. Just enough to stabilize |
| 4 | Head neutral | Ears over shoulders. Don't crane or tuck chin |
| 5 | Screw shoulders out | External rotation — pits forward. Creates stable socket |

**Key insight:** This organizes everything *together*. The problem with
fidgeting is you adjust shoulders without pelvis, lower back without
ribcage. The bracing sequence treats your trunk as one integrated unit.

**After you run it: hands off.** The position doesn't need to feel
perfect. It needs to feel *organized*. Trust it and move on.

## Step 2: Why Neutral Feels Hard

If you can't settle into the bracing sequence without effort, it's not
a willpower problem — it's a **restriction problem**. Tight tissue is
pulling you out of position, so holding it feels like work.

Common restrictions behind the fidgeting pattern:

| Symptom | Restriction | Area |
|---------|-------------|------|
| Shoulders feel wrong no matter where you put them | Tight T-spine (rounded upper back from sitting) | Area 1 |
| Over-retracting shoulder blades | Tight pecs pulling shoulders forward; you overcorrect | Area 3 |
| Lower back arching | Tight hip flexors tilting pelvis forward | Area 7 |
| Over-tucking pelvis to compensate | Tight psoas won't let lumbar spine relax into neutral | Area 5 |

## Step 3: Daily Mobilization (16 min)

Attack the restrictions that make neutral feel hard. 3 target areas
max per session. Go deep on fewer spots — don't scatter.

### The Session

| Time | Mobilization | Target | How |
|------|-------------|--------|-----|
| 4 min | Couch stretch (2 min/side) | Area 7 — hip flexors | Back knee against wall/couch, squeeze glute, stay upright. This is the single best undo for sitting |
| 4 min | T-spine smash (4 min total) | Area 1 — upper back | Lie on double LAX ball (peanut), arms crossed. Roll segment by segment from mid-back to upper back |
| 4 min | Pec smash (2 min/side) | Area 3 — anterior shoulder | LAX ball against wall on pec minor (below collarbone, toward armpit). Slow pressure wave |
| 4 min | Psoas smash (2 min/side) | Area 5 — trunk | Lie face down, LAX ball between navel and hip bone. Sink in, then slowly extend and flex the hip |

### Rules

- **2 min minimum** per position, per side. Less doesn't create lasting
  tissue change
- **Test/retest**: Run the bracing sequence before and after. If neutral
  feels easier, you hit the right targets. If no change, swap targets
  next session
- **No pain face**: Relaxed face while mobilizing. Grimacing tightens
  neck flexors and creates downstream restriction
- **Uncomfortable is fine, sharp pain is not**: Stand at the entrance
  of the pain cave, don't go in

### Progression

Once the daily session becomes routine and you notice neutral getting
easier, rotate in these based on what's still restricted:

| Mobilization | Target | When to add |
|-------------|--------|-------------|
| Banded shoulder distraction (2 min/side) | Area 2 — posterior shoulder | Shoulders still feel jammed |
| Hamstring smash with barbell (2 min/side) | Area 9 — posterior chain | Lower back still rounds when hinging |
| Adductor smash with roller (2 min/side) | Area 8 — medial chain | Knees collapse inward in squat |
| Calf smash on roller (2 min/side) | Area 12 — calf | Ankle dorsiflexion limited |

## Tools You Need

**Essential (start here):**
- 3 lacrosse balls (tape 2 together as a peanut for T-spine work)
- Resistance band (Rogue Monster Band or similar)
- A wall or couch (for couch stretch)

**Nice to have:**
- Foam roller
- Softball (larger surface for quads, glutes)
- Kettlebell (psoas compression, shoulder capsule work)

## Healthy-Day Upgrade

The FoundMyFitness Starrett episode adds the missing all-day layer:
posture improves fastest when the day contains enough movement inputs.
See [healthy-day-starrett.md](healthy-day-starrett.md).

Use this hierarchy:

1. **Reset once** with the bracing sequence.
2. **Mobilize the restriction** if neutral feels hard.
3. **Add movement snacks** so the body repeatedly touches useful shapes
   during the day: walk, floor-sit, squat, hang, breathe, rotate.

## The Mindset

> "All human beings should be able to perform basic maintenance on
> themselves." — Kelly Starrett

**Stop adjusting. Start mobilizing.** The fidgeting is your body telling
you it doesn't have the range yet. 15-20 minutes of daily tissue work
and the fidgeting will quiet down on its own. No days off — this is body
maintenance, not training.
