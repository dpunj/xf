# Sitting Posture — Seminal Resources

Quick-reference guide for desk workers. Books, videos, methods, and
cues for building awareness and holding good posture while sitting.

## Books

### Deskbound — Kelly Starrett

The desk-worker-specific companion to Becoming a Supple Leopard.
Covers workstation setup, the sit-stand-move cycle (reorganize every
10-15 min), and targeted mobilizations for undoing sitting damage.
Directly addresses hip flexor shortening, thoracic kyphosis, and
forward head posture.

**Key takeaway:** Sitting isn't the enemy — sitting in one position
without reorganizing is. Move between sitting, standing, and floor
positions throughout the day.

### 8 Steps to a Pain-Free Back — Esther Gokhale

Gokhale studied populations with near-zero back pain (indigenous
cultures, historical statuary) and reverse-engineered what they do
differently. 300k+ copies, subject of an RCT at Stanford. Different
lens than Starrett — anthropological rather than athletic.

**Two sitting techniques:**

| Technique | How | When to use |
|-----------|-----|-------------|
| **Stretchsitting** | Gentle traction against a chair back to lengthen the spine. Hips slightly forward of backrest, upper back uses backrest as anchor point | When you have a chair with a back — creates passive decompression |
| **Stacksitting** | Anteverted pelvis on a firm seat edge, spine stacks naturally on top of sit bones | Stools, benches, or edge-of-chair sitting |

### Back Mechanic — Stuart McGill

World's foremost spine biomechanics researcher (30+ years, University
of Waterloo). Grounded in actual measurement, not theory or tradition.

**Core insight:** Endurance matters more than strength for spine
health. The spine fails from repeated low-load flexion (sitting
slouched for hours), not from single heavy loads.

**The Big Three** — minimum effective dose for spine stability:

| Exercise | What it trains | Form note |
|----------|---------------|-----------|
| Curl-up | Anterior stability | Hands under lumbar spine, one knee bent. Lift head/shoulders barely off ground. No sit-up |
| Side bridge | Lateral stability | Elbow and feet (or knees for beginner). Straight line from head to feet. Hold, don't dip |
| Bird dog | Posterior stability + anti-rotation | Opposite arm/leg extended. Sweep hand over ground (don't touch), return. Square hips throughout |

**Programming:** Descending pyramid — 3 reps of 10s hold, then 2, then
1. Build hold duration over weeks. Total session: ~10 min.

## Methods

### Gokhale Method

Posture method based on anthropological observation. The two sitting
techniques above (stretchsitting, stacksitting) are the core of it.
Start with the book "8 Steps to a Pain-Free Back" or the free
introductory videos at [gokhalemethod.com](https://gokhalemethod.com/).

### Alexander Technique

A 130-year-old method for retraining unconscious movement habits.
Uses "inhibition" (pausing before habitual movement) and "direction"
(conscious intention without muscular effort). A BMJ randomized
controlled trial showed sustained benefit for chronic back pain.

**Best for:** The awareness and mindfulness side of posture. Addresses
the slumping you don't notice and the tension you carry without
awareness. Hard to learn from a book — 1-on-1 lessons with a
certified teacher (AmSAT or STAT) are the gold standard.

### Feldenkrais (Awareness Through Movement)

Somatic education: slow, exploratory movements to rewire motor
patterns. Think of it as debugging your movement — proprioceptive
awareness rather than forcing a "correct" position.

**Best for:** Developers. It's introspective and systematic. You're
running diagnostics on your movement patterns, not following a
checklist. Free "Awareness Through Movement" audio lessons are
available online.

## Quick-Reference Cues

Run through these as a single sequence when you sit down, then hands
off. These complement the bracing sequence in
[posture-reset.md](posture-reset.md).

| Cue | Source | What it does |
|-----|--------|-------------|
| Sit on your **sit bones**, not your tailbone | Gokhale | Anteverts pelvis slightly — lets spine stack naturally |
| Squeeze glutes to set pelvis | Starrett | Neutralizes pelvis — stops the arch/tuck hunt |
| Pull ribcage down over pelvis | Starrett, McGill | Stacks ribcage on pelvis as one unit — prevents rib flare |
| Belly tight at 20% | Starrett | Light abdominal engagement — just enough to stabilize |
| Lengthen the back of your neck | Gokhale, Alexander | Undoes the chin-poke. Think "puppet string from crown of head" |
| Ears over shoulders | Universal PT consensus | Corrects forward head posture — the single most common desk issue |
| Screw shoulders out (pits forward) | Starrett | External rotation — opens chest, sets shoulder in stable socket |
| Hinge at the **hips**, not the waist | McGill | When leaning forward to type, fold at hip crease, don't round the spine |

**The meta-cue:** If holding good posture feels like work, it's a
restriction problem, not a willpower problem. Mobilize the restriction
(see [posture-reset.md](posture-reset.md)) rather than white-knuckling
the position.

## If You Only Do Three Things

1. **Run the bracing sequence** every time you sit down
   ([posture-reset.md](posture-reset.md))
2. **Do the Big Three** once a day — 10 minutes for spine stability
3. **Reorganize every 30 min** — stand, move, sit differently. The
   best posture is the next posture

## Starrett Healthy-Day Add-On

From [healthy-day-starrett.md](healthy-day-starrett.md): do not solve
posture by adding more cues. Add movement inputs.

- Before laptop: 5-minute walk, hip spin-up, shoulder spin-up, or breath
  spin-up
- During passive time: floor-sit and rotate positions for 20-30 minutes
- During work: use movement snacks instead of shoulder/pelvis fidgeting
- For desk shoulders: check ribcage/breath/T-spine before micromanaging
  scapulae
