# Becoming a Supple Leopard — Kelly Starrett

Distilled reference for AI-assisted mobility coaching.
Source: Becoming a Supple Leopard (2013), Dr. Kelly Starrett & Glen Cordoza.

## Core Model

Every movement fault is either a **motor-control error** (you can get into
the position but don't) or a **mobility restriction** (you can't get into
the position). Fix motor control first. If the position is still
unattainable, it's a mobility problem. Treat it with the three systems
below.

## Foundational Principles

### 1. Bracing Sequence

The universal setup for every loaded movement. Perform in order:

1. Squeeze glutes to set pelvis in neutral
2. Pull ribcage down (lock ribcage to pelvis)
3. Belly tight (engage abs ~20% — not max bracing)
4. Set head in neutral (ears over shoulders)
5. Screw shoulders into stable position (external rotation)

This creates a **braced neutral spine** — the non-negotiable starting
position. If you lose it mid-movement, reset.

### 2. One-Joint Rule

The spine is not a motor. It transmits force. All movement originates
from the primary engines:

- **Hips** — flexion, extension, rotation
- **Shoulders** — flexion, extension, rotation

If someone bends from the lumbar spine instead of hinging at the hip,
they've broken the one-joint rule. The spine should remain organized
(braced neutral) while the hips and shoulders do the work.

### 3. Laws of Torque

Stable joint positions are created by generating rotational force:

- **Shoulders / arms**: default to **external rotation** torque (screw
  hands out on a bar, turn pits forward on a push-up)
- **Hips / legs**: default to **external rotation** torque (screw feet
  into the ground, shove knees out in the squat)

Exception: feet in a pulling stance (deadlift setup) use slight
**internal rotation** of the feet to load the posterior chain, but the
knees still track out.

Torque creates stability. If you can't generate torque in a position,
you're either missing range of motion or have a motor-control error.

### 4. Tunnel Concept

Every nerve, artery, and muscle passes through soft-tissue tunnels on
its way to the extremities. A restriction anywhere in the tunnel
compromises everything downstream. Tight T-spine → restricted shoulder
→ elbow/wrist problems. Tight hip capsule → knee pain → ankle
restriction. Always check **upstream and downstream** of the pain site.

## Movement Hierarchy

Three categories organized by complexity. Master each before
progressing.

### Category 1 — Stable to Stable

Start and finish in a position of high stability (PHS). Midline stays
organized throughout the entire range.

| Movement | Archetype |
|---|---|
| Air squat | Squat |
| Back squat | Squat |
| Front squat | Squat |
| Overhead squat | Squat |
| Deadlift | Hinge |
| Box squat | Squat |
| Strict press | Press |
| Bench press | Press |
| Dip | Press |
| Pull-up | Pull |
| Chin-up | Pull |

### Category 2 — Stable → Dynamic → Stable

Add a speed or disconnect element. You leave the PHS momentarily, then
return.

| Movement | Archetype |
|---|---|
| Wall ball | Squat + press |
| Push-press | Press |
| KB swing | Hinge |
| One-arm swing | Hinge |
| Jumping/landing | Squat |
| Kipping pull-up | Pull |
| Rowing | Hinge + pull |
| Snatch balance | Squat + press |

### Category 3 — Full Dynamic

Maximum speed, multiple transitions, highest motor-control demand.

| Movement | Archetype |
|---|---|
| Burpee | Full body |
| Clean | Hinge → squat |
| Hang clean | Hinge → squat |
| Jerk (push / split) | Press + dynamic |
| Snatch | Hinge → overhead squat |
| Muscle-up | Pull → press |

**Coaching rule:** Faults in Category 2/3 almost always trace back to
Category 1 positions. If someone's clean is ugly, check their deadlift
and front squat first.

## Three Mobility Systems

Work through these in order when diagnosing a restriction.

### 1. Joint Mechanics (Capsule)

**What:** The joint capsule — a thick ligamentous sac around every
joint — can get tight and restrict range of motion. Sitting for hours
adaptively shortens the anterior hip capsule. Rounded shoulders tighten
the anterior shoulder capsule.

**How to treat:**
- **Banded distraction** — wrap a band around the joint and let it pull
  the surfaces apart, creating space for the joint to move
- **Flexion gapping** — jam a towel/ball behind a knee or elbow to create
  compression that decompresses joint surfaces
- **Forced position + rotation** — force the joint into a good position
  (e.g., KB floor press pushing humerus into socket), then rotate
  through internal/external range

**Rule:** If you skip joint capsule work, you're leaving a huge chunk
of restriction on the table.

### 2. Sliding Surfaces (Tissue)

**What:** Skin, fascia, nerves, muscles, and tendons should all slide
freely over each other. Sitting, repetitive movement, and training
cause layers to mat down and stick together, creating an "internal
cast." No amount of stretching fixes this — you need shear force.

**Test:** Press two fingers on the back of your hand and move the skin
in all directions. That's how your tissues should move everywhere. If
the skin over your knee, thigh, or ankle doesn't slide freely, you
have sliding-surface dysfunction.

**How to treat:**
- **Pressure wave** — full bodyweight on a ball or roller, move slowly
  to work through knotted tissue. Sink and wait, don't rush
- **Smash and floss** — tack down on a tight spot with a ball, then
  move the limb through full range of motion

### 3. Muscle Dynamics (Length/Tension)

**What:** Shortened or stiff muscles that lack range. This is the last
system to address — if joint mechanics and sliding surfaces are clear,
most range-of-motion issues resolve without traditional stretching.

**How to treat:**
- **Contract and relax (PNF)** — build tension at end-range for 5s,
  release, move into new range for 10s. Repeat
- **Banded flossing** — band around joint for distraction while moving
  through full-range loaded positions (e.g., banded split squat for
  hip flexors)
- **Position-specific mobilization** — always mobilize in a shape that
  resembles the position you're trying to improve (e.g., mobilize in
  the bottom of the squat to improve the squat)

## Mobilization Methods

Seven techniques. Most can be combined. Mix and match within a session.

| Method | System | What It Does |
|---|---|---|
| **Pressure wave** | Sliding surface | Full weight on ball/roller, move slowly across knotted tissue. Sink into deepest layers |
| **Contract and relax** | Muscle dynamics | PNF: 5s tension at end-range, release, 10s in new range |
| **Banded flossing** | Joint + muscle | Band creates distraction while you move through full ROM in a loaded position |
| **Smash and floss** | Sliding surface | Tack on tight spot with ball, then move limb through full ROM to unglue layers |
| **Paper-clipping** | Joint + muscle | Oscillate in and out of end-range/peak tension. Small rhythmic movements, not static holds |
| **Voodoo flossing** | All three | Wrap compression band around joint/tissue, move through full ROM for 2-3 min, unwrap. Blood flood on release restores sliding surfaces and joint mechanics simultaneously |
| **Flexion gapping** | Joint mechanics | Towel/ball jammed behind knee or elbow to decompress joint surfaces and restore flexion range |

## Programming Rules

### Daily Minimum

15-20 minutes per day, every day. No days off. This is body
maintenance, not training.

### Session Structure

1. **Prioritize painful joints and tissues first** — spend the first
   portion of your 15-20 min on pain/restriction
2. **Then work positions of restriction** — mobilize for the positions
   you're training that day (e.g., squat day → ankles, hips, T-spine)
3. **Then undo positional damage** — reverse whatever your workday did
   (sitting → open hip flexors, couch stretch)

### Dosing

- **2 min minimum** per position, per side. Less than 2 min doesn't
  create lasting tissue change
- **3 target areas max** per session. Don't scatter across 10
  positions — go deep on fewer
- **Test/retest** every time. Squat before → mobilize → squat after.
  If no change, wrong target area

### Priority Order by System

1. Motor control (free — just fix the movement pattern)
2. Joint capsule (banded distraction before training)
3. Sliding surfaces (pressure wave, smash and floss — before or after
   training)
4. Muscle dynamics (contract/relax, banded flossing — after training)

### Sample Session (16 min)

**Goal: Mobilize the shoulder**
- Shoulder capsule mobilization: 2 min each arm (4 min)
- Overhead banded distraction: 2 min each arm (4 min)

**Goal: Mobilize bottom of squat**
- Single-leg flexion with external rotation bias: 2 min each side
  (4 min)

**Goal: Undo sitting**
- Super-couch mobilization: 2 min each side (4 min)

## Rules of Mobility

1. **Test and retest** — observable, measurable results or switch
   targets
2. **If it feels sketchy, it's sketchy** — uncomfortable is fine,
   sharp/hot/burning pain means stop. Stand at the entrance of the
   pain cave, don't go in
3. **No days off** — body maintenance has no rest days
4. **Don't make a pain face** — relaxed face while mobilizing. Grimace
   shortens neck flexors, creates downstream restriction
5. **Make mobility realistic** — mobilize in positions that approximate
   what you're training (couch stretch > lying hamstring stretch)
6. **Always mobilize in a good position** — don't overextend or
   collapse while mobilizing. Keep the bracing sequence
7. **Explore your business** — don't just follow the photos. You know
   where you're tight. Informed freestyle

## Tools

**Essential (everyone should own):**
- 3 lacrosse balls (2 taped together as a double ball / peanut)
- Resistance band (Rogue Monster Band or equivalent)
- Foam roller

**Nice to have:**
- Voodoo floss band (or bicycle tire tube cut in half)
- Softball (larger surface for quads, glutes)
- Barbell (calf smash, hamstring smash)
- Kettlebell (shoulder capsule mobilization via floor press)

## Key Quotes

> "All human beings should be able to perform basic maintenance on
> themselves."

> "If you sit for prolonged periods of time, you're not going to get
> change by mobilizing for 2 minutes before a workout."

> "The upstream-downstream approach is that you don't need to know
> anything about movement or anatomy to take care of yourself. Just
> target the muscle and tissue above and below the restricted area."
